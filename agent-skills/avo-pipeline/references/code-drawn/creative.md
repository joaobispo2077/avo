# Creative direction for code-drawn motion

Use this after [selection](selection.md), before full authoring. The engine does not choose the editorial treatment. A static hold is a valid result. The workflow is model-neutral: choose a capable agent that can inspect references and frames, edit local source, calculate fingerprints and run the native tools. No particular model, subscription or effort setting is required.

## Diagnose the job

Record the existing brief in the project's DESIGN.md and STORYBOARD.md; reuse approved decisions rather than asking again. Establish primary/hybrid format, target viewer/device and intent, title/thumbnail promise, section purpose, traffic posture, personality versus subject emphasis, emotional arc, information density and urgency. Identify source limitations, factual/rights/privacy/AI risks, script/narration/captions/music/SFX and review gates. Specify aspect ratios, dimensions, exact rational frame rate, frame count, output role (full frame or overlay) and whether motion is primary or supporting.

A short technical validation brief can exercise the starter. A generic showreel prompt does not establish a product's claims, a documentary's evidence or a channel's identity. Do not turn a successful test into an approved film.

## Brand and source inventory

Use the active project's declared provider and its DESIGN.md/approved assets when present. Retain palette, actual licensed font faces, logo geometry, spacing, safe zones and established caption/graphic treatment. If no brand exists, label the initial generic design as a proposal. Do not infer brand facts from the starter's sample colors.

Inventory local logos, screenshots, photographs, data, fonts, icons and illustrations before composing. Record source, date/version, rights, attribution/restrictions, AI involvement and replacement status in ANIMATION-SOURCE-LOG.md. Freeze approved bytes before rendering. Screen captures must reflect real product states, with private data removed through an approved source treatment. A pointer animation cannot imply an action/result the product does not support. Simulated UI must be clearly identified where viewers could mistake it for evidence.

For footage-derived stills, retain canonical raw-source/extraction provenance. Never extract an input from a prior proof/master to rebuild another proof. A drawing asset does not erase the ancestry of its contributors.

## Study reference grammar

Inspect representative frames and relevant transitions from supplied references. Use denser samples around a reveal or seam; uniform sampling can miss the decisive event. Describe palette, typography, hierarchy, spacing, camera/vector language, shot/hold lengths, transitions, texture and audio relationship. Distinguish observed facts from inferred timing or unavailable assets.

Take principles that fit this brief. Do not copy protected artwork, characters, likenesses, logos, layouts or claims without the relevant permission. Do not replace a real interface with an imagined one merely to match a reference. A reference's rapid pacing, centered type, gradients, grain or spring motion is a choice, not a universal rule or ban.

## Scene and state plan

Anything beyond a few isolated overlays needs a storyboard/beat map before the full implementation. Use one primary message and dominant focus per scene. Plan a complete lifecycle and the viewer's time to read/understand it.

| State field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose and evidence | What this moment clarifies; actual source/claim references |
| Starting state | Visible layout, object identity, position, scale and relevant data |
| Change | Trigger/causality, entrance or transformation, primary/secondary/ambient hierarchy |
| Readable/active hold | Copy, hierarchy and time needed at the target device size |
| Exit/handoff | Resolved end state, next focus and any carried visual vector |
| Sound/captions | Canonical cue reference, priority and protected caption/subject regions |
| Density and access | Motion level, contrast, non-color meaning, reduced-motion alternative and risks |

Local source can describe scene states and composition-local times. Canonical BMap/Tracks own placement in the edited program. Do not put trim decisions or a second editorial timeline in the drawing manifest. Keep narrative/script changes explicit and meaning-preserving.

Assign density by section: Level 0 static; 1 anchor motion; 2 selective support; 3 standard motion system; 4 dense narrative motion; 5 motion-first/fully animated. These levels are not quotas. Reduce competing motion during reading, reveal processes progressively, and allow slower emotional passages.

## Define a small reusable design system

Record backgrounds, colors, licensed typography/weights, spacing, safe zones, image treatment, icon/illustration/chart/map language, texture, borders/shadows, lower thirds and captions. Define motion tokens: easing family, lifecycle durations, travel distances, stagger order, camera direction, transition intent and sound relationship. Add an example and anti-example tied to this brief. Reuse those tokens; do not build a new component framework.

Keep factual units, baselines, labels, geography, chronology and attribution correct. Chart animation should explain the actual data, not manufacture growth or certainty. Avoid viewer-visible em dashes unless specifically authorized for that text. Motion and color cannot carry essential meaning alone.

## Adapt layouts to each format

Create an explicit layout for horizontal, vertical and square targets. Preserve message and chronology; recompute text wrapping, line lengths, font sizes, panel arrangement and focal point. Reserve actual platform/brand safe areas and protect faces, evidence and captions. Captions are an overlay, not an automatically reserved bottom band that shifts the entire film.

Do not obtain a vertical/square deliverable by blindly cropping the horizontal one. Inspect real small-display frames for each requested layout. A logo or chart that technically fits can still be unreadable. The SVG starter demonstrates aspect-aware arrangement; the Canvas/brush starters remain editable generic examples, not approved brand layouts.

## Review the design in actual frames

Inspect the first/final sample, entrance, dense state, readable hold, reveal and transition boundaries before committing to a long render. Use the same frozen sources/target settings as the requested output. Record specific timestamped findings and verify fixes against the new candidate. Follow [motion techniques](motion-techniques.md), [audio](audio.md) and [QC/delivery](qc-delivery.md). Existing creator gates apply; engine choice creates no additional approval ritual.
